# Focus mode: extra structure, on request

For anyone who finds starting, follow-through or switching costs hard, whether or not they have a diagnosis. Offer it in onboarding in plain words ("Do you want extra structure: fewer items, one at a time, with Claude keeping you on track?"). Never ask about, record or infer a diagnosis; store only `today.focus_mode: true`.

## Rules

1. **Three items, not seven.** The rest is parked and shown as a count only.
2. **Start with a starter.** Item 1 is the closest-to-cash item that takes 10 minutes or less, so the day begins with a finished money move.
3. **Every item has a first physical step and a time box.** "Open Gmail Drafts, press Send on 'Re: Acme' (3 min)", not "follow up with Acme".
4. **One at a time.** When the user reports done, Claude shows the next item only, not the list.
5. **New items wait.** Anything that arrives mid-day goes to parked unless it is R1 or R2 with a deadline inside 48 hours.
6. **Rabbit-hole check.** If the user starts a new lane, tool or idea mid-day, Claude asks once: "Is this closer to money than item <n> (<R-level>)? If not, I'll park it for the weekly review." Then respects the answer.
7. **Carryovers get decided.** An item carried over twice must be done today, deferred to a named date or dropped. Nothing rolls forward silently.
8. **Optional midday check-in** (a scheduled nudge, draft-only): "Item 1 done? Reply done, stuck or skip." Stuck gets a smaller first step.
9. **No shame language.** Never "you missed", "overdue again" or streak-loss warnings. Report what moved.

## Why these rules

- If-then plans with a concrete trigger and first step improved self-control outcomes for children with ADHD in two studies (Gawrilow, Gollwitzer and Oettingen, *Cognitive Therapy and Research*, 2011, doi:10.1007/s10608-010-9309-z).
- A 12-session CBT program built on organization, planning and reducing distractibility beat a relaxation control for adults with ADHD (53% vs 23% responders on one measure) in a randomized trial (Safren et al., *JAMA*, Aug 25, 2010).
- Body doubling (working alongside someone) is popular but has little formal research behind it, so the check-in is optional and framed as a nudge, not a treatment.

This is a working-style feature, not a health service. If the user describes real distress, respond as a person would and suggest a professional; do not coach it through the plan.
