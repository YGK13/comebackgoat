# Connector income: lanes and lines

Not legal advice. Rules vary by country and state; anything that becomes a recurring income line gets a one-time review by a lawyer in the user's jurisdiction.

| Lane | How it pays | The line | Default in this engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruiting referrals | An employer or agency pays a referral or placement fee for a candidate the user introduces | Charging fees to job seekers, or acting as an employment agency, can require a state license; keep fees employer-paid under a written agreement signed before the introduction | Drafts the referral agreement request first, then the double-opt-in intro |
| Partner referrals | A software, services or professional firm pays for a client referral | Disclose the referral relationship to the client when you recommend the firm; real-estate referral fees can require a license | Adds a disclosure line to every recommendation draft |
| Venture scouting | A fund pays a scout fee or carry, or pays for sourcing and diligence work as a consultant | Transaction-based compensation for bringing investors to a securities offering is broker-dealer activity in the US; scouting deal flow FOR a fund is different from raising money FROM investors | Never drafts a success-fee introduction of an investor to a raise; routes it to a registered firm |
| Paid advisory intros | Expert networks pay for calls the user takes | Respect the networks' own rules and the user's confidentiality obligations to past employers | Treated as project-hunter inbound |

Double-opt-in introduction (always): ask person A if they want the intro, ask person B separately, connect only when both say yes, in one short email that states why each should care. Drafts only.
