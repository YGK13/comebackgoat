## 3. Stage A: Fill the cohort (Days -14 to -3)

1. **Post** the LinkedIn test post (comment "GOAT" or DM). Put the comebackgoat.com link in the first comment.
2. **Applications** arrive through the site form. Review within 2 business days.
3. **Screen** with these criteria:
   - **Accept:** 10+ years of experience at director level or above; out of work, leaving within 90 days or newly solo; can commit 30 minutes a day for 14 days; willing to contact former colleagues and bosses; has or will get a Claude Pro or Max plan; can request a LinkedIn data export.
   - **Red flags:** wants it done for them; refuses any outreach; active legal dispute with a former employer; hostile to AI; in acute crisis (refer to support instead); expects a guaranteed job.
4. **Fit call (20 minutes):**
   - 2 min: their situation in their words.
   - 5 min: what a win in 90 days looks like; what they have tried.
   - 5 min: how the sprint works: Claude does legwork, they press Send, you coach. Daily 30 minutes. Day 14 target.
   - 3 min: the money: $997 (or 2 × $549), effort guarantee, refund before kickoff.
   - 5 min: questions; then "Want a seat?"
   - Decline kindly when the fit is wrong, and say why in one sentence.
5. **Accept:** send the acceptance email (Template 1) with the payment link and the prerequisites checklist. Seat confirmed on payment.

## 6. Stage D: Profile review (Day 2, async, 20 minutes)

The participant forwards Claude's "Day 2: my profile for review" email. Check:
- [ ] Positioning line is specific (who, what outcome), not generic.
- [ ] Five strongest claims are true, sourced and impressive enough to open doors.
- [ ] Contradictions are resolved (titles, dates, numbers).
- [ ] Voice samples sound like them, not like Claude.
- [ ] Runway band and money appetite match what they told you.
Reply with up to 3 fixes. Do not rewrite their profile yourself; tell them what to ask Claude to change.

## 7. Stage E: Offer call (Day 3, 30 minutes, 1:1)

The most important call of the sprint. Bring Claude's Day 3 email (offer options, Top 10 targets, boomerang status, money found).

1. **Pick ONE offer (10 min).** Push for the option with the clearest buyer, the most recent proof and the shortest path to a yes. Kill vague offers ("strategic advisory").
2. **Set the price (5 min).** Floor: a project at no less than 2 to 3 weeks of former salary. Fractional: a monthly retainer sized to days per week. State a range; never undercut out of fear.
3. **Sanity-check the Top 10 (10 min).** Who is missing? Who is a waste of a message? Which former employer is the best boomerang?
4. **Commit (5 min).** "Tomorrow you send 5." Name the first 5.

## 8. Stage F: Move (Days 4 to 13)

### 8.1 Your daily oversight (5 minutes per participant)
Read each Day [n] report. Score each participant:

| Status | Rule | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Sent their minimum, streak intact | One-line encouragement or nothing |
| Yellow | Sent less than the minimum, or 1 day without a move | Same-day message: "What's in the way of the first two items?" |
| Red | 2 working days without a move, or a report that says "stuck" twice, or zero replies after 15 sends | 15-minute call within 24 hours |

### 8.2 Group calls (Monday and Thursday, 45 minutes)
- 5 min: wins board. Every reply, call and offer gets named.
- 25 min: hot seats (2 people, 12 minutes each): one stuck point, solved live with Claude on screen when useful.
- 10 min: teaching moment, picked from what you saw that week (Section 10).
- 5 min: each person says their top 3 for the next 3 days.

### 8.3 Midpoint 1:1 (Day 7, 20 minutes)
Review the scorecard: sends, replies, reply rate, calls booked. Decide one change for week 2: different targets, a sharper message, a narrower offer or more follow-ups. Re-commit to sending at least one offer by Day 13.

### 8.4 Proposal and pricing support (as needed)
When a buyer asks for a proposal or a price, the participant sends you Claude's draft. Turn it around within 4 working hours: tighten scope, defend the price, add one proof point. This is where your experience earns the fee.

## 9. Stage G: Day 14 review (45 minutes, 1:1)

1. **Scorecard (10 min):** what moved, what worked, which channel produced replies.
2. **Next 30 days (15 min):** the refined offer, the next 25 people, weekly targets, engines to keep running.
3. **Case study interview (10 min, recorded with permission):** before, what they did, result so far, one sentence they would tell a peer.
4. **Next step (10 min):** pick one of the options below:
   - **Continue:** monthly membership (group calls plus the engines) when available.
   - **Authority Stack:** the done-for-you build for those ready to scale.
   - **Effort guarantee:** if they qualify, another 14 days free.
   - **Graduate:** they keep running the protocol on their own.
Ask for a testimonial and two referrals (Template 6).

## 10. Intervention playbook (what you will see, and what to do)

| Pattern | Sign | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| Not pressing Send | Drafts pile up; streak breaks | "Send the two smallest ones now while we're on the phone." Turn on focus mode. |
| Perfectionism | Rewrites every draft | Set a rule: edit one sentence max, then send. Remind them drafts are already in their voice. |
| Fear of looking foolish | Avoids former colleagues | Reframe: they are asking for advice, not a job. Most people like being asked. Start with the warmest 3. |
| Vague offer | Can't say who buys or what it costs | Rerun Section 7 with one buyer and one outcome. If still stuck, pick for them and test for a week. |
| Price fear | Wants to quote low | Anchor to former weekly pay and the buyer's cost of the problem. Quote a range; let the buyer react. |
| All job, no business | Only wants applications | Fine if that is their goal; keep warm outreach as the main channel because referrals beat applications. |
| Zero replies | 15+ sends, nothing back | Check targets (too senior or too cold?) and message (too long, unclear ask). Switch to referral asks. |
| Overwhelm or distress | Shuts down, emotional reports | Slow down; human first. Suggest professional support when warranted. The sprint can pause. |
| Tech blocker | Connector or export fails | Switch to the upload path; never let tech eat more than one day. |

## 12. Guardrails for you as coach

- You never log into a participant's accounts. They forward reports; Claude works inside their own account.
- Nothing goes out in a participant's name without their press.
- You are not their lawyer, accountant or therapist. Flag; refer; do not advise on legal terms of separation agreements.
- Keep everything a participant shares confidential; case studies only with written permission.
- Record calls only with consent.

## 13. After the cohort

- Day 30 check-in (15 minutes each): results since Day 14, testimonial if not yet given.
- Cohort retro (1 hour, solo): reply rates by channel, where people got stuck, protocol changes. Change the protocol file first, then run the next cohort.
- Metrics to track across cohorts: completion of calibration by Day 2, average sends, reply rate, % with a paid conversation by Day 14, % with an offer sent, revenue within 30 and 90 days, NPS, referrals.
